# Cascade City Bike-Share Program — Year One Report (January 2025)

## Program summary

Cascade City launched its municipal bike-share program in January 2024 with 1,200
bicycles (400 of them electric) across 95 docking stations, operated under contract
by Velocity Urban Mobility. The program's first-year budget was 3.8 million dollars,
funded half by the city and half by a title sponsorship with a regional bank.

## Ridership results

- Riders took 918,000 trips in year one, against a first-year target of 750,000.
- The average trip length was 2.3 kilometers and 14 minutes.
- Electric bikes were used 3.1 times more often per bike per day than pedal bikes.
- 22 percent of surveyed riders said bike-share replaced a car trip at least once a
  week, the program's headline sustainability claim.
- Ridership was heavily concentrated: the top 10 stations generated 41 percent of all
  trips, while the 15 lowest-performing stations averaged fewer than 3 trips per day.

## Problems

Vandalism and theft were worse than projected: 9 percent of the fleet was lost or
written off in year one, versus an industry-typical 4 to 5 percent. Rebalancing costs
(moving bikes from full to empty stations by van) consumed 31 percent of operating
spend. The report also notes an equity gap — only 6 of the 95 stations were placed in
the city's lowest-income quartile neighbourhoods.

## Year two plan

The council approved adding 300 electric bikes, converting 20 underused stations to
"virtual docks," extending service to two low-income districts with subsidized $5
monthly passes, and renegotiating the rebalancing contract with a per-trip incentive
structure.
