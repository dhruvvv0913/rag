# Atlas University Spaced Repetition Program — Evaluation Report (May 2025)

## Program overview

In the Fall 2024 semester, Atlas University launched a structured spaced repetition
program for introductory STEM courses, led by Dr. Rohan Mehta of the Learning Sciences
department. The program enrolled 1,842 students across four courses: Introductory
Biology, General Chemistry, Calculus I, and Introduction to Programming.

Participating students received daily review sessions generated by a modified SM-2
algorithm (the same family of algorithms used by Anki), tuned so that new cards and
reviews together required a median of 18 minutes of review time per day. Review items
were written by course staff and mapped one-to-one to that week's lecture objectives,
which Dr. Mehta's team argues is the main reason adherence stayed high.

## Results

Compared with a matched control group of 1,790 students from the previous two years:

- Final exam scores improved by 12.4 percent on average across the four courses, with
  the largest gain in General Chemistry (16.1 percent) and the smallest in Calculus I
  (8.9 percent).
- Six months after the courses ended, program students retained 71 percent of tested
  material, versus 44 percent for the control group — the difference the report calls
  "the headline result."
- Program completion was high: 91 percent of enrolled students were still doing reviews
  in the final week of the semester, and the dropout rate was 9 percent.
- The cost of running the program came to 23 dollars per student for the semester,
  most of it staff time for writing review items.

## Caveats

The report is explicit about limitations. Students self-selected into the program, so
motivated students are likely over-represented despite the matched-control design.
Gains were measured on exams that reward recall; the report does not claim transfer to
design-heavy or open-ended work. Finally, the 18-minute daily commitment proved hard to
sustain for students juggling jobs: dropout among students working more than 20 hours
per week was 2.4 times the average.

## Recommendation

The evaluation committee recommended expanding the program to all first-year STEM
courses in Fall 2025, with a reduced 12-minute daily target for working students, and
a follow-up study on long-term retention at the 24-month mark.
